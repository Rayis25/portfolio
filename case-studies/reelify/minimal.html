A shoppable-video app was losing people before they ever uploaded a video.

Reelify lets Shopify brands add TikTok-style shoppable video to their product pages. The concept was strong; the onboarding wasn’t. I ran a UX and microcopy audit — the wireframe-level fixes and the words — to close the gap between install and first upload.

About

Reelify is a Shopify app: install it, and shoppers can buy directly from a video playing on your product page.

A strong concept was losing users at the door. Onboarding friction, unclear value delivery and no in-app engagement loop meant many merchants never got past their first session — so the video feature never got the chance to prove itself.

Onboarding audit

Display Style Selection: one screen, six things doing the work

Early in onboarding, merchants choose how their videos will appear — Stories, Carousel, Grid or List. It’s a small decision with an outsized effect on drop-off if it feels irreversible or overwhelming.

Header, with reassurance

“Choose Your Display Style” plus “you can change later” — sets context and removes the fear of a permanent choice.

Four style cards

Arranged in a row for quick side-by-side comparison, instead of one option at a time.

Preview placeholders

Visual boxes inside each card, so recognition doesn’t depend on reading every label.

Primary action: Next

One clear way forward, bottom-right, doing the one job of moving onboarding along.

Secondary action: Skip for now

An escape hatch from choice overload — present, but never competing with the primary path.

Centred, spacious layout

No clutter competing for attention on a screen a first-time user sees once.

Suggested next

Three low-effort additions

None of these were broken — they’re proposed refinements for wherever the screen hasn’t already caught up to them.

  • A “Step 2 of X” indicator — so the screen states its place in the sequence instead of feeling open-ended.
  • A “Recommended” badge on one option, to give undecided users a default rather than four blank choices.
  • Icon-based previews in the placeholder boxes, hinting at the layout difference even before real content exists.
Microcopy

Rewriting the words between install and first upload

Wireframes fix structure; they don’t fix a welcome screen that undersells the product. The audit’s second half was a line-by-line rewrite of the copy carrying a merchant through their first session.

Welcome screen

“Let Customers Shop While They Watch”

“Add a video, tag your products, and let shoppers buy without leaving the screen.”

Create My First Shoppable Video

Generic SaaS onboarding leans on words like “get started” that could belong to any product. This headline is visual and specific to what Reelify actually does — it names the experience the merchant is switching on, not the app category.

Three onboarding steps, written to be skimmed in seconds:

01

📹 Add your first video from your phone, computer, or YouTube.

02

🏷️ Tag the products you want customers to shop.

03

🚀 Publish to your store and turn browsers into buyers.

Empty state — no videos yet

“Your store is waiting for its first shoppable video.”

“It takes less than 2 minutes to set up. Start now.”

Add My First Video
Success — video published

“Your Shoppable Video is Live 🎉”

“Customers can now watch and shop. Track performance in your dashboard.”

View in My Store

The empty state personifies the store and reassures on effort before asking for anything. The success message celebrates the milestone, then hands the merchant straight to their next action instead of leaving them idle on a static confirmation screen.

Retention

The uninstall flow answers the reason it was actually given

Most cancellation flows show the same message regardless of why someone is leaving. This one branches on the merchant’s stated reason, so the response is relevant rather than generic.

If they sayReelify responds
“Hard to use”“Looks like setup was tricky. Want a 10-minute guided walkthrough?”
“Too expensive”“We can offer a smaller plan or extended trial. Want to see options?”
“Using a different app”“Here’s how we stack up, just in case you want to compare before leaving.”

Dashboard microcopy

  • Upload button — “+ Add Video to My Store”
  • Analytics empty state — “No data yet. Your first video will light up this dashboard.”
  • Help link — “Need help? Chat with us. We reply fast.”

Why branch the copy at all

Personalising the response to the stated reason for leaving makes it feel like someone read the answer, not like a form was submitted into a void. That alone tends to move people from “leaving” to “willing to hear more.”

Expected impact

What this was projected to move, going in

This audit was delivered as a report and a walkthrough, not a shipped release — the figures below are the projected impact the recommendations were built against, not measured results.

  • A clearer welcome screen raising activation rate
  • A more motivating onboarding flow producing more first-video uploads per install
  • Guided empty states reducing drop-off mid-journey
  • Context-aware retention copy reducing churn at the uninstall step

Every word in the product should work as hard as the features do.
