A shoppable-video app was losing people before they ever uploaded a video.

Reelify lets Shopify brands add TikTok-style shoppable video to their product pages. The concept was strong; the onboarding wasn’t. I ran a UX audit — five key screens, a full heuristic evaluation, and a microcopy rewrite — to close the gap between install and first upload.

About

Reelify is a Shopify app: install it, and shoppers can buy directly from a video playing on your product page.

A strong concept was losing users at the door. Onboarding friction, unclear value delivery and no in-app engagement loop meant many merchants never got past their first session — so the video feature never got the chance to prove itself.

Approach

Flipping the question to find where it actually breaks

The standard framing — “how do we get users to finish onboarding” — is too comfortable to surface real friction. Every screen in this audit started from its opposite: what would make someone quit right here?

Comfortable question

How do we get users to finish onboarding?

What would make a user quit halfway?

They don’t see the ROI, can’t preview outcomes, or get stuck with no content to work with.

Comfortable question

How do we show layout choices?

What would confuse a non-technical store owner?

Icons are vague, there are no visual examples, and nothing guides them toward what works best.

Comfortable question

How do we explain product tagging?

What would make someone skip this step?

Tagging feels technical and unimportant, with no visual cue for what it earns you.

Three passes: audit, review, and a report the team could act on

The brief was deliberately narrow — find where onboarding was actually losing people, not a redesign of the whole app.

Key screens

Five screens, audited the same way every time

What’s already working, where it actually breaks, and the specific fix — for every screen between install and a merchant’s first live video.

01

Onboarding — Import Videos

Working
  • A clear CTA and a logical entry point for setup
  • Minimal distractions on the first screen a merchant sees
Issues
  • No ROI framing — doesn’t say why the tool matters
  • No demo content for merchants without videos yet, and no social proof
Fixes
  • “Convert viewers into buyers — stores see up to 3× more engagement.”
  • A sample-video fallback, plus a trust badge (“Trusted by 2,000+ Shopify stores”)
02

Display Style Selection

Working
  • Layout choices are named simply, and the choice is reassured as reversible
Issues
  • Emoji icons stand in for real previews, and nothing recommends a default
Fixes
  • Real screenshots or short preview clips in place of icons
  • A “Recommended” mark on one option, so undecided merchants have a default
03

Product Tagging

Working
  • Video on the left, product selection on the right — feels native to timeline editing
Issues
  • “Scrubber” is jargon; the dropdown has no product images
  • No confirmation once a tag is placed, and no marker for where tags exist on the timeline
Fixes
  • “Tagged videos get 2.8× more clicks” — said once, where it’s earned
  • Product thumbnails in the dropdown, visible tag markers, a quiet “Tagged” confirmation
04

Enable in Theme Editor

Working
  • Instructions are broken into steps with a clear intent
Issues
  • No visual of Shopify’s own theme editor; “best section” is vague
Fixes
  • A short embedded clip of the actual placement flow
  • Concrete suggestions — “below the hero image,” not “best section”
05

Pricing

Working
  • Free-versus-paid is direct, and the free plan keeps the entry barrier low
Issues
  • No CTA to upgrade; unclear what happens past the free plan’s view limit
Fixes
  • A direct upgrade CTA, plus a short FAQ on hitting the free-plan limit

Microcopy

Rewriting the words between install and first upload

The heuristic audit fixes structure; it doesn’t fix a welcome screen that undersells the product. The second half of the audit was a line-by-line rewrite of the copy carrying a merchant through their first session.

Welcome screen

“Let Customers Shop While They Watch”

“Add a video, tag your products, and let shoppers buy without leaving the screen.”

Create My First Shoppable Video

Generic SaaS onboarding leans on words like “get started” that could belong to any product. This headline is visual and specific to what Reelify actually does — it names the experience the merchant is switching on, not the app category.

Three onboarding steps, written to be skimmed in seconds:

01

📹 Add your first video from your phone, computer, or YouTube.

02

🏷️ Tag the products you want customers to shop.

03

🚀 Publish to your store and turn browsers into buyers.

Empty state — no videos yet

“Your store is waiting for its first shoppable video.”

“It takes less than 2 minutes to set up. Start now.”

Add My First Video
Success — video published

“Your Shoppable Video is Live 🎉”

“Customers can now watch and shop. Track performance in your dashboard.”

View in My Store

The empty state personifies the store and reassures on effort before asking for anything. The success message celebrates the milestone, then hands the merchant straight to their next action instead of leaving them idle on a static confirmation screen.

Retention — leaving the app

The uninstall flow answers the reason it was actually given

Most cancellation flows show the same message regardless of why someone is leaving. This one branches on the merchant’s stated reason, so the response is relevant rather than generic.

If they sayReelify responds
“Hard to use”“Looks like setup was tricky. Want a 10-minute guided walkthrough?”
“Too expensive”“We can offer a smaller plan or extended trial. Want to see options?”
“Using a different app”“Here’s how we stack up, just in case you want to compare before leaving.”

Dashboard microcopy

  • Upload button — “+ Add Video to My Store”
  • Analytics empty state — “No data yet. Your first video will light up this dashboard.”
  • Help link — “Need help? Chat with us. We reply fast.”

Why branch the copy at all

Personalising the response to the stated reason for leaving makes it feel like someone read the answer, not like a form was submitted into a void. That alone tends to move people from “leaving” to “willing to hear more.”

Retention — high-intent moments

Before someone leaves to compare, not after

The uninstall flow catches people on their way out. This is earlier — the moment someone is quietly weighing Reelify against an alternative, before they’ve decided anything.

What was already there

  • A clean UI with clear structure
  • Informative feature sections
  • Trust-building elements already in place

Suggested additions

  • An exit-intent moment — “Before you go, here’s what people like you found helpful”
  • A micro feedback prompt — “Is something missing for you?” with three tap options
  • Real testimonials with context — “We chose this tool over [X] because…”
  • Softer CTAs than “Cancel” — “Need help deciding?” or “See why others switched”

Expected impact

What this was projected to move, going in

This audit was delivered as a report and a walkthrough, not a shipped release — what follows is the projected impact the recommendations were built against, not measured results.

  • A clearer welcome screen raising activation rate
  • Real previews and a recommended default reducing decision fatigue at the layout step
  • Tagging copy and visual feedback improving completion of the tagging step
  • Context-aware retention copy reducing churn at the uninstall step and before it

Every word in the product should work as hard as the features do.
