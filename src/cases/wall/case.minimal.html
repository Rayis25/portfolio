About

Wall connects people inside one neighbourhood through useful, time-bound posts and a local store layer. Posts live for 24 hours and disappear.

No archive, no scroll trap, no performance pressure. Two surfaces define it: a community feed where neighbours share real-time information — lost pets, road closures, parking changes, recommendations — and local store listings, where nearby shop owners post time-limited offers in exactly the same format as everyone else.

Product vision
Strategy

One principle underneath every decision: social familiarity

Social familiarity is the feeling of a natural, low-stakes neighbourhood interaction. Not a broadcast. Not a performance. Not a competition for attention. Everything below is downstream of that one idea, and three commitments carry it.

01

Calm by default

The experience should never feel urgent or algorithmically manipulated. People open the app when it is useful to them — not because the product trained them to check compulsively.

02

Authenticity over polish

Posts don’t need to look good. A quick photo of a broken streetlight beats a beautifully designed announcement. The UI has to feel comfortable for unproduced sharing.

03

Utility drives engagement

People return because the app was genuinely useful — not because of a badge, a streak or a like count. Retention is earned through real-world relevance.

Principles

Six rules the interface has to obey

Principles are only useful if they can settle an argument. Each of these is written so a designer can hold a screen against it and get a yes or a no.

Neighbourly tone

Language, prompts and empty states speak like a calm neighbour, not a startup. No exclamation marks pushing you to post.

No performance pressure

No public like counts, no follower numbers, no leaderboards. Posts are seen because they are useful, not because they went viral.

Ephemeral by design

The 24-hour window is a feature, not a limitation. It removes the anxiety of permanent posting and keeps the feed relevant to right now.

Graceful emptiness

An empty feed is a quiet street, not a failure state. Zero-content states are handled with calm rather than aggressive nudges.

Low-commitment participation

Reacting, saving or sharing is one quiet tap. The bar to take part should feel lower than social media, not higher.

Stores are neighbours too

Store listings are part of the community, not advertising. Owners are neighbours with useful information, and the UI reflects that equality.

Anti-patterns

The strategy is mostly a list of things we refused to build

Most of the engagement mechanics in social products are well understood and easy to copy. The harder work was writing down which ones we would not ship, and what we would do instead — so the decision was already made before anyone felt pressure to hit a number.

Industry pattern — not builtWhat we do instead
Push notifications baiting users back with vague urgencyNotifications only for direct relevance — a reply to your post, or a saved store offer. Silent by default.
Streak mechanics and posting remindersNo streaks, no cadence pressure. The 24-hour window is about freshness, not urgency. Posts expire quietly.
Public engagement metrics on every postEngagement is private or hidden. Authors may see view counts privately; nothing is shown to others.
Infinite scroll and algorithmic rankingThe feed is chronological, geographically local and finite. When you reach the end, you reach the end.
Onboarding that guilts you into completing a profileName and neighbourhood. That’s enough. Everything else is optional and non-blocking.
Empty states that nudge you to create content“Quiet on the street right now” — never “Be the first to post!”
Promoted placement and pay-to-boost listingsEvery store listing uses the same format regardless of how active or paying the owner is.
Onboarding

Three screens, and nothing is asked of you until the last one

The anti-pattern list rules out onboarding that guilts people into completing a profile, so the entry sequence had to earn its length. Each screen carries exactly one idea — what the app is, who it is for, and what you can do with it — and the account only appears at the end.

What the composer deliberately leaves out

  • No floating “Create” button demanding attention — just a quiet inline field
  • Media is optional and never prompted; attach a photo only if it helps
  • Category tags exist but are never required
  • Confirmation is a small toast, not a celebration animation

What browsing deliberately leaves out

  • No algorithmic sorting and no option to inflate the radius to feel busier
  • No sidebar, related posts or recommendations on an opened post
  • One quiet reaction and a “Useful” flag — no comment threads that become stages
  • Store owners see only their own post views, never competitor data
Notifications

Only send it if the user would thank you for it

Notifications are the most common way social products build unhealthy loops, so the policy is written as a closed list. Anything not on it does not get to send.

Reply to my postDelivered once, no re-ping if unread. Quiet push, no badge count.
Saved store offer expiringOne gentle reminder two hours before expiry, and only if opted in for that store.
Weekly neighbourhood digestOptional, off by default. Plain text, no images, no engagement bait.
New post in feedNever. People open the app when they choose to.
Streak or posting reminderDoes not exist in this product.

Language

Copy is where a calm product usually breaks

A product can hold every structural principle and still sound like a growth team wrote it. So the strategy specifies the sentences, not just the rules.

Don’t writeWrite instead

Be the first to post today!Nothing new right now — check back later.
You’re on a 5-day streak!No streak UI exists at all.
Share with your neighbours!Post if it’s useful.

Measurement

Choosing metrics that don’t reward manipulation

Time-in-app, DAU and post volume all reward the exact patterns the product refuses to build. If those stayed on the dashboard, they would eventually win the argument. So the strategy names both lists.

Measured
  • Useful interaction rate — share of posts receiving at least one “Useful” reaction
  • Post relevance — posts from the user’s exact neighbourhood versus out of area
  • Voluntary return rate — opens that happen without a push notification
  • Store offer redemption — people who visit a store after seeing its post
  • Session completion — opening, reaching the natural end of the feed, and closing
Deliberately not optimised
  • Session length — longer is not better
  • Notification click-through — more clicks means more manipulation
  • Posts per user — we do not want pressure to post more
  • Daily active users as a vanity number, unqualified by usefulness
Governance

A checklist so the strategy survives contact with a roadmap

Strategy documents get filed and forgotten. This one ends in eight questions that run before anything ships, because the pressure to add a streak arrives long after the principles are written.

For every decision, ask: would a helpful neighbour do this? If the answer is no, redesign.

  • Does this create urgency that doesn’t exist in real life?
  • Does this surface a public metric that could become a performance stage?
  • Does this notification serve the user, or serve our retention numbers?
  • Could someone feel embarrassed, pressured or judged by this interaction?
  • Does this push people toward more consumption than is useful?
  • Does this treat store owners unfairly against community members?
  • Does the empty state make the product feel broken or guilt-inducing?
  • Is any default setting tuned for our metrics rather than user comfort?
Summary

A utility with social warmth — not a social network with a utility layer

Wall earns its place on a phone by being genuinely useful a few times a week. It succeeds when someone opens it, finds something relevant in two minutes, and closes it satisfied.

The experience should feel the way a good neighbourhood feels: low drama, high trust, occasional helpfulness, no pressure. People share what’s useful because it helps their neighbours — not for likes, not for reach, not for a streak.

The app respects your attention. It will not beg for your time.
