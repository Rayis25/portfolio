Product Design UX Writing 0→1

An onboarding flow has to earn a habit before the product has proven anything.

Wisdom is a note-taking app I’m building myself — a second brain for turning what you learn into something you can actually find and use again. I designed its onboarding end-to-end: the sequence, the questions, and every word on the way from install to a first captured note.

About

Wisdom turns things you read, hear, or learn into notes you’ll actually go back to — the personal-knowledge-management idea, built as a mobile app instead of a folder structure.

A note app has no feature to demo on day one; there’s nothing in it yet. The only thing onboarding can sell is the habit itself, so the six screens between install and the empty home screen carry almost all the weight of whether someone comes back.

Approach

Belief before data, and an exit on every screen

Three decisions shaped the sequence, before a single word of copy was written.

01

Sell the idea, then ask for anything

The welcome screen states the premise; the very next screen is a single quote with no button but Continue. It exists to make someone agree with the idea before being asked a single question about themselves.

02

Personalisation is optional, visibly

“Skip personalisation” sits in the corner of the first question screen and stays reachable through the rest of the flow. Asking for a name and habits is worth doing, but not worth gating access behind.

03

End on the one action that matters

The flow doesn’t end on a summary or a dashboard. It ends on a single oversized add button and one line of copy — “Add to your wisdom” — because capturing a first note is the only thing that predicts a second one.

The flow

Six screens, each doing one job

03

What should we call you?

The first question is the lowest-friction one there is — a name, not a preference or a commitment. It also puts “Skip personalisation” in view for the first time, before asking anything that might feel like an interrogation.

04

How often do you make time to learn?

Two options, not five. A frequency slider or calendar picker would model this more precisely, but precision isn’t the point during onboarding — a fast, confident tap is.

05

How are you currently managing your notes?

This is the one answer worth designing around later — someone coming from physical notes needs a different first-run nudge than someone arriving with none at all. Captured now, used post-launch.

06

How did you hear about us?

Attribution, asked last and asked plainly — it tells the product nothing about how to help this person, so it earns the least prominent spot in the sequence, right before the reward of landing on the home screen.

Content pass

A line-by-line pass over the onboarding copy

Structure was the first pass; the words got a separate one. Small phrasing fixes, made before this flow ships.

Before
After
“How do you heard about this app”
“How did you hear about us?”
“Freinds”
“Friends”
“How often do you involve in learning”
“How often do you make time to learn?”

None of these change what the screen does — they change whether it reads like a finished product or a form someone forgot to proofread. For an app whose entire pitch is taking knowledge seriously, that reads louder than it should.

Status

What’s next

Wisdom hasn’t launched, so there’s no completion-rate or retention number to report here honestly. The onboarding flow above is built and in the app now; next is usability testing with people outside my own head, before it ships.

The onboarding is the only feature a note app gets to demo on day one.
